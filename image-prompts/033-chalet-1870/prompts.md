# 033 · CHALET 1870

**Category:** Real estate  
**Concept:** A chalet built in 1870 above a Swiss alpine village, restored and for sale; a seasonal tour and a viewing request.  
**Website:** /showcase/chalet-1870/  
**Images:** 10 generations, 19 images  
**Image status:** New art-directed prompts (27 Nov): generate these with Seedream 5.0 Pro

## Creative direction

One house, one hundred and fifty winters. The story moves from blue dusk in deep snow to firelit rooms, steam from a hot tub and skiers coming home; one summer meadow shot shows it is a house for every season. The photography is calm and tactile: wool, blackened timber, stone, snow on a sleeve.

## Visual identity

- **Look:** alpine winter photography: blue snow light, warm amber windows, wood smoke and falling snow, 35mm, quiet luxury, photorealistic.
- **Palette:** snow white, sun-blackened larch, granite grey, wool red and amber.
- **Type and layout cues for the website:** old-style serif with heavy contrast, set small and precise; a single wool red accent.

## Consistency

Every prompt that shows the chalet repeats this description word for word, so Seedream draws the same one each time. Do not edit it:

> a large three-storey alpine chalet built in 1870 with a granite stone ground floor, upper storeys of sun-blackened larch timber, a wide low-pitched roof deep in snow, carved wooden balconies, small square windows glowing amber, a wood-fired hot tub on a stone terrace, standing alone on a snowy slope above a village in the Swiss Alps with a pine forest behind

- Generate 01 first; keep the chalet description word for word.
- Winter shots share blue dusk light and amber windows; the summer shot keeps the same chalet in a meadow.
- Every prompt ends with the same look and palette sentence, so the set matches.
- People are always clearly different individuals, often seen from behind or with faces out of frame, because Seedream repeats one face when people are not described apart. Never a real person, a brand or a logo.
- Grids (2x2) give four pictures from one generation; Claude cuts them into four files.
