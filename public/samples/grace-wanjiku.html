01 · For parentsWhat happens on the day

The day of the operation, step by step.

Knowing what comes next helps children and parents feel calmer. This is what a typical day looks like for a planned operation; your team will explain anything that is different for your child.

  1. Morning

    Arrive

    Check in, change into soft clothes, and meet the nurse who stays with you.

  2. Before

    Meet the team

    The surgeon and the anaesthetist explain the plan and answer every question.

  3. Asleep

    Falling asleep

    A parent can usually stay until your child is asleep, with the favourite toy.

  4. Operation

    The operation

    You wait nearby. Someone from the team updates you when it is finished.

  5. Waking

    Waking up

    You are there when your child wakes. Feeling sleepy or grumpy is normal.

  6. After

    Home

    Many children go home the same day, with clear instructions and a phone number.

02 · QuestionsThe ones parents ask most

No question is too small.

Will my child feel pain during the operation?+

No. Your child will be fully asleep, under the care of a specialist anaesthetist. Afterwards some discomfort is normal; the team gives pain relief and checks on it regularly.

Can I stay with my child?+

Usually one parent can stay until your child falls asleep, and you will be with them again when they wake up. The team will tell you if anything is different on the day.

When can my child eat and drink again?+

It depends on the operation. Before surgery there are fasting times to follow for safety; after surgery the nurses will tell you when your child can start with small sips.

How do I explain the operation to my child?+

Use simple, honest words and keep it short: what will happen, that they will sleep through it, and that you will be there when they wake. Our play team has picture books that help.

What should we bring?+

Comfortable clothes, any medicines your child takes, the favourite toy or blanket, and your list of questions.

This page explains what usually happens. It is not medical advice: always follow the instructions from your child's own care team.
