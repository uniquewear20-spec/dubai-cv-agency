01 · The journeyOne container, door to door

Ships are rarely the problem. The waiting happens on land.

Follow one container from a factory to a shelf. At every handover someone is waiting for information somebody else already has.

  1. 01 · Factory

    Packed, sealed, booked.

    The booking says Tuesday. The truck company hears about it on Monday night.

    Waiting for: a truck slot
  2. 02 · Port gate

    A queue at the gate.

    The driver waits while the terminal checks a booking reference typed in by hand.

    Waiting for: a gate appointment
  3. 03 · Yard

    Stacked, then moved twice.

    Boxes for the same ship are spread across the yard because nobody knew the loading order in time.

    Waiting for: a loading plan
  4. 04 · At sea

    The easy part.

    Three weeks across two oceans, on time to within a day. Then the ship arrives at a busy port.

    Waiting for: a berth
  5. 05 · Customs

    One missing document.

    The box is cleared in hours if the paperwork is right, and in days if one form is not.

    Waiting for: a signature
  6. 06 · Warehouse

    On the shelf.

    The software's job is to make every one of these waits visible early enough to shorten it.

02 · The softwareThree things it does

See it. Predict it. Move it.

  1. See

    Every box, one screen.

    Positions from ships, terminals, trucks and customs, joined into one timeline per container.

  2. Predict

    Arrival times that update.

    A range, not a promise, and it narrows as the cargo gets closer. Planners get warned while there is still time to act.

  3. Move

    Plan B before it is needed.

    When a route or a port closes, it proposes the alternatives with their cost in days and money.
