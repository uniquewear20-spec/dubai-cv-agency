I run a 90-seat restaurant in Dubai. Follow one Friday with me, from the four o'clock line-up to the last light off at one.

Follow the night ↓

16:00Line-up

Fifteen minutes that decide the night.

Every service starts the same way: the whole team, front and back of house, around one table. Three things, every day.

  1. The book

    Who is coming tonight: birthdays, allergies, first visits, regulars, and the table that was unhappy last time.

  2. The tasting

    Everyone tastes tonight's specials, so nobody describes a dish they have not eaten.

  3. One thing better

    One detail from yesterday that we will do better tonight. Just one, so that it actually happens.

17:30Doors open

The first table gets our best.

어서 오세요

Eoseo oseyo: come in, welcome. The words every Korean restaurant greets you with. What I teach is the feeling behind them: you were expected.

  1. 0:30

    Greeted at the door within thirty seconds, by name if we know it.

  2. 1:00

    Seated, with coats and bags looked after before anyone has to ask.

  3. 2:00

    Water on the table before the menus.

  4. 5:00

    A word from someone who knows tonight's menu, not a script.

19:30Full room

A full room is a tide.

Guests never arrive evenly. I plan the night around the waves: who is on the floor at eight, when the kitchen gets a breath, and which tables we keep back for walk-ins.

21:30A difficult table

When it goes wrong, go first.

  1. 21:34

    A birthday cake leaves the pastry section for the wrong table.

  2. 21:35

    We tell the right table before they notice, and the wrong one with a smile.

  3. 21:43

    Pastry makes a new cake. The chef carries it out herself.

  4. 21:58

    They stay for coffee, and book their anniversary on the way out.

An illustrative story.

23:00Last orders

After last orders, it is a business.

  • The numbers

    Every week, line by line.

    Sales, labour and food cost, reviewed with the head chef and finance, so that decisions are made on facts.

  • The team

    Forty-six people.

    Hiring, training, rotas, and the conversations nobody wants to have, front and back of house.

  • The record

    Nothing is forgotten.

    Every review answered within a day, and every regular's preferences written down for the next visit.
